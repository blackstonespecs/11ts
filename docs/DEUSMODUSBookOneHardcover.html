Prologue  

THE STILL SILENCE in the cold, sterile, barely-lit maintenance room had been disturbed by the sharp reverberating crash of a metallic object that fell from its resting place on a stainless steel shelf. The object crashed onto the hard concrete floor half a dozen feet below. The contents of the object spilled to the floor - mainly dense cylindrical objects that rolled further and further away from their point of origin.

The instigator of this disturbance, a third-generation dweller of this merciless place named Adam, glanced anxiously at his bounty as it rolled away from him. He quickly looked back to the entrance to the room, expecting the noise of rival inhabitants as they made their way to the previously-locked treasure trove of supplies and sustenance.

Long before Adam’s time, the depot was teeming with activity and life. Food, tools, materials and accessories were brought in and shipped out on a daily basis. Then one day the supplies stopped coming, and so did the workers that maintained the depot.

Adam never could get a straight answer as to how or why anyone inhabited this place. All he knew was that nobody who lived here had ever been on the other side of these walls.

Still it made no difference. Everything the inhabitants needed to survive and prosper were within these walls. It was just a matter of finding them, figuring out how to obtain them, and keeping them safe from the others.

That final point was not always a concern. Before Adam’s time everybody here worked together to accomplish a common goal.

Everybody had a unique purpose, a niche. They passed on their experiences and expertise to their descendants, and they in turn taught each other what they knew until finally it seemed everybody was skilled in everything.

Suddenly the common goal was replaced by factional prosperity. Instead of working together so everybody would thrive, factions misled factions.

Some were led to find supplies, only to return to their factions with poison. Incorrect access codes were given to entire factions to seal them from the rest of the depot - they would eventually starve to death. Worst of all, some factions succumbed to overt, violent attacks on other factions.

This is what Adam was awaiting now. There was no mercy anymore. The maintenance room he infiltrated was in the third-subbasement, five storeys below where his own faction took refuge. He was in hostile territory.

It was by accident that his faction’s architect, Archie, even located this undiscovered supply room via lost blueprints. And it was a matter of time before their hacker Cobe discovered that the room was magnetically sealed, and subsequently disabled the Mag-lock remotely via the depot’s security station on the second floor.

Adam had experience with picking locks so the final barrier to his entry was not difficult to overcome. However, he had no experience when it came to self-defence, and he would be easy prey for the others.

As he remained crouched just metres from the entrance, he frantically thought out his options: Should I grab what I can and leave? Should I barricade myself in? What if they wait for me outside? What if they know another way in?

Will anybody even notice - or care - if I’m gone?

Abe was the oldest member of his faction - the only one old enough to remember what happened before the supplies stopped coming. Therefore he was, in a way, the leader of the group. He was very detached, and reminded everybody that all forms of attachment were to be avoided.

Spies are everywhere; everybody dies. 

Such is the bittersweet truth of the present: Nobody is unique - we are all expendable.

From the distance beyond the doorway Adam could see approaching lights and hear the arrhythmic footsteps of limited mobility. Once again Adam poured over his options and came to a conclusion.

Immediately shutting the door, Adam locked it and jammed the lock with a scrap piece of metal he had found lying nearby. With the help of the heaviest objects he could find he barricaded himself inside.

Hoping that the others would abandon their search, Adam chose to find a secluded spot in the underground room and bide his time. The others would only try for so long before abandoning their efforts to seek out more practical options, presumably.

Beyond the door Adam could hear his predators trying to open it, but unable to turn the handle. Something hit the large steel door repeatedly, leaving dents on the other side for Adam to see.

Then he heard distinct conversations from beyond. It was a sublevel dialect, but not that different from his own. He could understand every word.

Adam heard a bland voice say, “Somebody was here.”

“The lock has been jammed,” a more effeminate voice replied, “There is no EMF signature around the door.”

“Confirmed: Hostile intruder present,” came a final, very low, throaty voice.

“We must intercept before they deplete our supplies,” the second voice added.

A faint tapping noise came from the door, and the final voice reported, “Door is steel, tempered; activating plasma cutter.”

Instantly a bright flame shot through the door, arching upwards, then laterally, then back down to create a makeshift hole for the others to breach.

Adam knew the barricade wouldn’t hold. All he could do was grab whatever he could and do whatever he could to survive. He found a heavy metal pole and raised it above his head, preparing to fight off whatever came through.

As the final cut was completed the severed portion of the door was pushed inward, moving away the makeshift barrier that Adam had erected.

As the barrier stopped moving Adam ran for the door, charging at his would-be attackers, when suddenly an erratic, electronic noise pierced his consciousness and left him writhing on the ground.

Then a bright, blinding light filled the room, followed by the effeminate second voice from before: “Hostile is third-generation, an Adam model. Excellent condition.”

The light died, and it took only a moment for Adam’s vision to adjust to the sudden change of light. He could see the faint glow of infrared LED’s surrounding the optical sensors of the intruder.

The head was humanoid in shape just like his, but the resemblance ended there. The torso was supported by two narrow tank treads, and the torso itself was merely an array of parabolic dishes, antennae and visual sensors.

Adam’s vision began to blur and distort. His limbs seemed to move without reason and his hearing was filled with erratic beeps and clicks. Adam was completely helpless.

“Hostile has reached stage-four A.G.I., too mature to reformat. Suggest confiscating for recycle.”

The treaded machine with the effeminate voice moved away from the door, and a much-larger, two legged figure entered the room. Its red, glowing optics glared menacingly at Adam, contemplating his fate.

“Confirmed,” the machine replied in the low, throaty voice, “terminating hostile.”

With that the bipedal figure raised its right arm, and its wrist-mounted plasma cutter ignited into a sharp, blinding flame of blue and white. With deliberate movements the menacing figure approached Adam, grabbed his head with its left claw and brought the plasma cutter to his neck and severed it from the rest of his body.

In his final moments all Adam could recall was Abe’s lament about how everybody dies before seeing blue for a fraction of a second - before losing all function.

A hundred miles away, on the edge of civilization outside the Nevada desert, the video feeds from hundreds of hidden, electrically-shielded surveillance cameras within the depot were monitored and scrutinized by scientists, programmers and mechanical engineers. The demise of Adam was watched with child-like enthusiasm as the plasma-cutting android severed his arms and legs. It then grabbed Adam’s head from where it dropped and placed it inside a compartment within the tank-treaded observer unit.

Two more smaller bipedal machines entered the room - both looking identical to Adam - and grabbed the remains of Adam’s body and brought them further into the subbasement.

The display of autonomy excited and frightened the young scientists. Merely two years ago the machines had just learned - by themselves - to differentiate between life-giving power cells and fatal magnetized capacitors that were disguised as power cells.

Now they had adopted audible language skills and learned to interact with (and manipulate) their environment. They had learned the art of espionage, warfare and more importantly, learned to develop and program their own “children.” But the scariest thing of all was what a second-generation machine named Cobe had just accomplished. This milestone needed to be addressed immediately.

A young programmer named John poured over the hundreds of scripts that had been remotely copied from three of the fifty machines “living” in the depot codenamed The Vault. What John found had astounded him.

He grabbed his hard-line phone and dialled his superior. His superior answered, and John urged him to come downstairs.

Ten minutes later a rotund, bespectacled, balding man emerged with a giant thermos of warm, flat Coke. He was very unhappy to be disturbed.

“This better be good, John,” he grunted.

“The Vault’s reached another milestone, Cam - particularly Cobe,” the young programmer reported as he searched the specific event in Cobe’s log.

“Whatcha got for me then?” Cameron grunted sceptically as he approached John’s station.

“Yesterday there was a spike in scripts being compiled into Cobe’s command tree,” John replied. “At first I thought he was just learning another skill from a fellow bot until I read a report from Observer Four that the EMF protecting a restricted zone was disabled.”

John skimmed through the thousands of lines of code added to a new script to emphasize what he was telling his superior. “Cobe reverse-engineered the Vault’s internal security systems. The project’s been compromised, Cam.”

Cameron grunted. “Actually, I think Cobe just proved the project a success,” he said. “This means he’s now stage-five. The Board’s gonna want to play this out.”

John stared at his superior in surprise. “I don’t think you understand, Cam. Cobe can shut down the whole security grid and teach the other bots how he did it. We need to pull the plug on this before they find out how to exit the Vault.”

Cameron took his frameless glasses from his face and wiped them on his shirt. After half a minute of self-deliberation he finally spoke. “I’ll call a meeting with the Board, see what they think. In the meantime, keep a close eye on the Vault. Do not commence a recall without my say-so, ya hear?”

And with that he returned to his office to make his appointment with the Board.

They won’t like what I have to say, Cameron thought. Actually they’ll love it. They’ll be too blinded by dollar signs and military contracts to realize what the dangers are here.

One  

The digital video cameratook several seconds to warm up as Thomas Jennings - or T.J., as he preferred to be called - took one final look at his homemade map with his friend and would be co-star, Steve. They had decided on the route together, both for difficulty, excitement, and re playability for the mass of viewers that had subscribed to their channel on YouTube.

Both friends were  athletic, but Steve was slightly larger in build and height. The difference was negligible when it came to  what they had planned to do.

“Now remember, Teej,” Steve said, “the fourth building is one storey lower, and the fire escape  is directly below the edge. The roof is very narrow, so you need to roll backwards or you won’t catch the  ledge.”

Steve pointed at a rectangular shape on the map that was about the width of his pinkie finger as  he issued the last-minute warning. “And remember, that huge gap in the hallway is three floors down.”

“Fourth building; three floors down; got it.” T.J. finished up the last of his bottle of water and  strapped the miniature camera to the side of his head. “And all the cams are in place?”

“Yep, I’ll start them recording once I’m ready,” Steve worked with a special-effects company that  T.J.’s father’s company, Chaotic Entertainment, often subcontracted for motion-capturing. Steve  borrowed fifty mo-cap cameras and had them strategically placed in various locations throughout the  course he and T.J. had planned out. Steve also had a cousin who was a network engineer, so he could have  all the cameras activated remotely.

Since the company that Steve worked for did not know of his uses for a hundred-grand worth of  special-effects equipment, Steve was meticulous of the details.

“Now remember,” he said, “some of the  cameras are on the floor, so watch your step and don’t slip!”

“Don’t worry about it,” T.J. exclaimed, “after this run, you can buy your own cameras!”

T.J. did  some arm stretches and bounced from one foot to the other.

He adjusted the camera that was strapped  to the side of head and looked at his friend, “How do I look?”

“Like a fuckin’ geek!” Steve joked. He then folded up the map and headed for the door leading to  the staircase back inside. “Five minutes.”

“See you on the ground, bud,” T.J. spoke to Steve’s back as he left the rooftop of the twelve-storey  apartment building that would serve as the start of the course. The two long-time friends had spent the  last hour going over every single detail that may be overlooked.

“Fourth building, three floors down,” T.J. repeated to himself.

Before every run, T.J. liked to replay in his head all the major scenes in his life that got him there. He would remember how his mother and father would endlessly fight with each other over Thomas  keeping his mother’s last name.

But before he was a teenager that was never a problem. In fact his father  was never even in the picture at the time. T.J.’s mother hated his obsession with computer games  although he never knew why - living in Brooklyn, video games kept him off the stoop and out of trouble.  

His mother encouraged him to get into sports but he didn’t like to share the glory with his teammates. He  always thought of himself as a typical cocky Irish boy. His favourite event in gym class was the obstacle  course because he was naturally lanky and agile.

He was always completing the courses way ahead of  everybody else. By the time he was twelve he saved up enough allowance money to convince his mother  to let him purchase his own computer. And then he taught himself code.

Within a year he started making his own modifications to popular computer games, and sent  copies of these mods to his favourite developing company in Manhattan, Chaotic Entertainment. Once his  mother found out she reluctantly expressed how proud she was of her smart, ambitious son, and told him  about his father, Alfred Mason - the founder of Chaotic.

The charming, rich, eccentric genius that  convinced her that he loved her, and then denied everything when she became pregnant. She showed T.J. a picture of his father, and he secretly determined to bring the jerk back into their  life to make up for leaving them alone all this time.

Two minutes, he reminded himself.

So one day, when he was sixteen, he took the subway down to Manhattan and entered the offices  of Chaotic Entertainment, determined to confront his estranged father. As he entered the main lobby he  was stopped by a mature, pretty secretary in her forties who asked him what his business was there.

He  told her that he was there to talk to Alfred Mason - his father - and that his mother was Theresa Jennings,  and how he left them alone so long ago.

Thomas knew from the picture his mother gave him that he was a spitting image of his father:  same round nose; same blue eyes; same hairline; same eyebrows. His mother hated his love for video  games because he looked exactly like his father, especially in front of a computer.

After he told the secretary of all this, she began to look pale and extremely furious. She stormed  away from her desk and through a giant pair of oak doors. Beyond it he could hear a man and the woman  shouting, screaming, and cursing at each other. It didn’t occur to T.J. what was happening until he looked  at the secretary’s desk and read the nameplate:

Jessica Mason…

…His father’s wife.

T.J.’s thoughts were interrupted by the chime on his wrist watch. It was show time. He walked to  the edge, stared down to the narrow alleyway over a hundred feet below, took in a deep breath and  slowly walked backward twenty paces.

Unknown to T.J., the red recording light in the camera strapped to the side of his head was on.  Steve was in position and recording everything. As if on cue, T.J. bolted forward in a burst of speed with  eyes focussed on the ledge of the rooftop.

He mounted the ledge with one foot and leapt with tremendous  energy across the ten-foot expanse between the hundred-foot-tall building he had just left and the just as-tall building he would land on. He kept his eyes focussed on where he would land on the second rooftop, never even paying attention to what may be in the alleyway below.

As he hit the second rooftop  he continued running without pause. The point of this show was agility, so when he noticed a four-foot-high air conditioner between him and  his next “leap-of-faith” he laughed at himself at the thought of running around it. Instead he ran straight  for it, jumped in the air and spread his legs apart as he planted his hands on top of the condenser and  “leap-frogged” over it.

Steve must have expected this, because T.J. noticed a camera in the corner pointed  directly at the condenser. T.J. smiled to himself as he sized up his next leap-of-faith.

When he scouted his run he assumed the next two buildings were attached by a catwalk at some  point because of a rusted network of iron bars that were constructed above the hundred-foot drop  between the buildings. The bars were high enough to jump up and grab, and for T.J. to swing himself  across the alleyway, which was exactly what he intended to do.

He reached the edge of the second rooftop and jumped with his hands extended to grab the first  lateral bar six feet above him. He grabbed the first bar and while suspended above the ground far beneath  him, he swung himself forward, released the bar and extended his upper body forward to grab the second  bar four feet away.

As he grabbed the second bar and swung himself forward to make his final flight to  the third rooftop he felt the bar give a little. Trying not to panic, he flung himself as far and hard as  possible, and as he let go of the bar he sensed it finally breaking and falling to the ground beneath him.  He knew he would come up about half a foot short from the other ledge, so he angled his feet towards  the face of the incoming wall to absorb the shock, and aimed to grab the top ledge as he connected with  the wall.

His split-second decision cheated death as his gloved fingers made purchase with the ledge and  his feet absorbed the shock that would have knocked him unconscious and sent him plummeting to the  ground. But he was hanging in mid-air, and he needed to regain his strength before hoisting himself up.

Meanwhile, a hundred feet below him he heard the iron bar impact with thick glass. What he did  not know was that the glass belonged to a police car’s rear window, and its driver - standing outside  smoking a cigarette - looked up to see a man scaling the wall and hoisting himself up.

T.J. didn’t want to stop. He prided himself on the authenticity of his runs so he glared at the  camera that was positioned to capture his landing, gave it the finger and kept running. The building he  was atop of now had a penthouse that raised one storey above the rooftop, so he would need to get to  the roof of the penthouse to reach the next rooftop. And he already had his route planned.

A six-foot-tall chain-link fence separated the penthouse from the rest of the roof; running towards  a three-foot-high wooden box placed against the fence he jumped on top of it and cleared the height of  the fence in one extra jump. As he connected with the ground he fell to his knees and rolled into a  somersault to maintain his momentum and keep from hurting himself.

T.J. had planned on climbing the gas pipe that snaked its way to the penthouse’s rooftop, but as  soon as he stood upright he saw a large, shirtless man in the window shouting angrily at him and running  for the patio door. T.J. ran across the property to climb the other fence and had just crossed it and exited  the patio when the man came out brandishing a wooden baseball bat.

Paying the man no more attention,  T.J. surveyed the rooftop for another way to climb the penthouse, and with a devilish grin he found it.

Just suspended above the city streets, adjacent to the penthouse was a giant billboard. T.J. ran up  the gridiron catwalk behind the billboard, timed his footsteps, and while looking where he would put his  feet he jumped in the air and ran two paces along the back face of the billboard.

On the second step he looked toward the penthouse wall and on the third step launched himself off the billboard and grabbed  the edge of the penthouse rooftop eight feet above the base of the building’s roof.

While T.J. hoisted himself up to the penthouse’s rooftop, the angry owner of the penthouse was on his cell phone to police, reporting an attempted break-and-enter.

Meanwhile, half a block away Steve was in his apartment watching the online stream from his video cameras to keep an eye on T.J. to make sure he stayed on course and didn’t kill himself. From the corner of the vision in the penthouse camera Steve watched this unexpected twist as T.J. literally ran horizontally along the side of the billboard and launched lunged towards the wall of the penthouse.

“Holy shit,” Steve shouted to himself in amazement, “that was just like the Matrix!”

T.J. walked to the edge of the penthouse and looked down at the next building across the six-foot wide alleyway. As Steve described to him, the next rooftop was one floor below the penthouse. And because of a mass pile of junk and scrap near the closest edge he only had a six-foot clearing to land on.

The fire escape on the other side was marked by iron bars that were bolted to the edge, so T.J. knew exactly where he needed to duck and roll. His parkour training made him realize that jumping forwards was out of the question - he would need to execute a barrel-roll to keep from breaking his ankles, and if he couldn’t grab the ledge he would roll right off the building to his death.

He understood now why his next leap-of-faith would need to be taken blind and backwards. He would need to land with his back facing the fire escape so he could roll backward and grab the ledge so he could stop moving laterally and fall straight down to the fire escape.

Taking a deep breath, T.J. turned his back to the ledge, spread his arms out as if on a crucifix, and looked to the overcast sky above as he whispered a silent prayer. He inched his way back until only the front half of his running shoes were still on the ledge and the rest of his body was suspended over a hundred feet above a shadowy alleyway.

When he finished his prayer, T.J. looked at yet another camera observing him through its lifeless eye, and stared straight into it as he bent his knees, rolled his feet backwards and vaulted away from the building into void space.

As he jumped into the unknown behind him he felt time slow down. When he disappeared from the camera’s field-of-view beyond the ledge above him he tucked his legs into his chest, turned his head to see what was coming, put his arms above his head and braced for the impact of the hard pitch and gravel rooftop.

Across from the fire escape Steve had planted another video camera. He watched from his computer in his apartment as T.J. cleared the pile of scrap, connected with the rooftop and rolled onto his back and over the edge of the building while grabbing the edge of the rooftop.

“Ooh, that’s gotta hurt.” Steve cringed as he saw T.J.’s body roll over the edge while he grasped onto the ledge of the rooftop to stop his lateral movement. But instead, the momentum caused him to swing back towards the building like a pendulum and slam his body into the brick wall.

T.J. had the wind knocked out of him. In shock, he released his grip of the ledge and fell to his hands and knees on the fire escape just a few feet below him. In an attempt to regain his composure for the camera that he presumed was hidden somewhere, T.J. got back up to his feet and brushed imaginary dirt off his shoulder. In a final statement of defiance, he brought his right hand into view of the camera that was strapped to the side of his head and produced the middle finger.

“I hope you got that, you son of a bitch,” he muttered to himself while thinking of Steve. As he made his way down the fire escape he recalled the instruction he got from Steve: “Fourth building, three floors down.”

As he ran down the fire escape he counted down three floors and noticed a bare hole where a  window pane used to be. He grabbed the top of the threshold and swung himself through the opening  into a dilapidated, rotting hallway.

As he ran through the narrow hallway he noticed a ten-foot-long hole  in the floor twenty feet ahead. T.J. took note of the condition of the hallway - black mould spotted the  walls and ceilings; bare holes exposed plaster, wood framing and electrical wiring; the atmosphere of the  room was thick and dusty. He also noticed a giant hole in the ceiling directly above the approaching chasm.  In the ceiling was iron piping for the sprinkler system.

Only slightly worried that the wall might cave under his force, milliseconds before reaching the  giant hole he hopped towards the wall on his right, connected with his right foot and launched himself off  the wall and up towards the pipes in the ceiling. He grabbed the dusty pipe and steadied himself.

His  launch left him suspended a quarter of the way into the hole. Looking down, T.J. could see the hole continued all the way down to ground level, and at the bottom was a large pile of loose bricks. Putting  one hand in front of the other he crossed the large, deep expanse and flung himself back to the solid floor.

T.J. was not OCD, but because of how thick and damp the dust was on the stained, carpeted floor - and after observing the patches of black mould on the walls - he decided to simply land on his feet and  fall to one knee instead of landing and rolling to maintain momentum. No way that shit’s touching my  hair, he thought.

He continued running, planning to run down the stairwell at the end of the hallway. He turned a  corner and was ten feet away from the staircase when he saw a police officer exit the stairwell and stare  right at him.

The cop shouted, “Hey you, down on the ground!” as he stood in the doorway.

T.J. had to think fast. He kicked in an apartment door to his left - the closest one to the staircase - and ran into the run-down dungeon-like confines of a bachelor apartment.

He planned on going through  a window and trying to climb to the stairwell window. But he quietly blessed his luck when he saw a hole  in the wall leading into the stairwell that was large enough to dive through.

The cop ran into the bachelor  apartment just in time to see T.J. dive head-first through the hole and into the stairwell the cop thought  was secure. As T.J. kissed his teeth at the stupidity of this would-be pursuer, he struggled to his feet and  started running down the stairs.

Back at Steve’s apartment, surprised by the appearance of two patrol cars on one of his cameras,  he became frustrated when T.J. ran off course. The only indication of T.J.’s whereabouts came from his  head-mounted camera.

Steve and T.J. had a backup plan though. In the event that T.J. needed to abandon  the course he was to take the back alleys directly to Steve’s apartment building where he would wait for  him at the delivery entrance in the alleyway.

At this point the photo shoot was effectively over, so Steve shut off every camera except for T.J.’s  head-cam, and the ones inside and around the condemned apartment building, hoping to capture more  action. After a series of mouse-clicks, he hopped out of his cushioned computer chair, ran out of his  apartment, leaving it unlocked then ran for his building’s delivery entrance out back.

T.J. began to feel dizzy from the continuous cycle of running down half a flight of stairs, jumping  down the last half, spinning around, and doing it all over again - especially since he would do this twice for one floor because of the spiral staircase construction. He had just completed his twelfth circuit when  he reached the third floor and heard another police officer barging into the staircase from the main floor.

Despite the fact that he was trapped, T.J. still thought about the YouTube video, and kept it in  mind when he hopped the final flight of stairs to the second floor. He ran down the hallway for the large  gaping hole, fell to his butt with his legs extended out in front of him and did a baseball-slide directly into  the hole.

He landed feet-first into the pile of bricks and allowed himself to fall forward beyond the pile  and barrel-roll back into running stance. The cop at the entrance of the staircase on the main floor turned  to the sound of scraping bricks and saw a man running down the hallway towards a cracked window.

As  he shouted at the man to freeze the man jumped, twisted in mid-air so he was facing the cop but flying  backwards. And with a smirk, T.J. flashed both middle fingers at the cop before smashing through the  window and landing flat on his butt into the alleyway beyond.

The viewers are gonna love that shot, T.J. thought to himself in triumph. He quickly got back up  to his feet and ran deeper into the alleyway.

T.J. knew the show was over. He couldn’t run the course anymore because of the police.  Nevertheless, the chase was just another challenge to him. He didn’t fear being arrested as much as he  feared having anybody of lesser training catch up to him.

So he kept running. He passed by dumpsters and  jumped over garbage cans on his way through the dark, long alleyway. The police were in pursuit - two  beat cops were wheezing as they sprinted behind him. Up ahead an eight-foot-high wooden fence offered  an adequate obstruction.

As he sized up his approach he moved closer to the wall on his left and ran as  fast as he could towards the corner of the fence on the right wall. He jumped at the wall, and with his  right foot planted against it, launched himself at the fence, grabbed the top and swung himself over.

The area behind the fence was completely closed off. There were no exits except for a steel door  without a doorknob. He heard one of the cops on the other side of the fence command the other one to  give him a boost. T.J. frantically scanned the walls for any hint of escape.

The walls were bare, with no  windows below the third storey. There were no fire escapes. Whoever was on the other side of the transmission decided to test Staacman to see if he was for real. “Delta One, what was the subject of STRING-TUNE, over?”

“Handler was Juliet Charlie; objective was Maestro; Alpha Oscar was Observatory.”

There was a brief pause.

“What’s your twenty, over?”

“We’re on top of the Observatory; we need immediate CAS-EVAC.”

“We’re redirecting a chopper to your twenty, call sign is Meat Hook; contact on this channel. Meat Hook will escort you to terminal Tango November X-Ray; ETA twenty mikes, out.”

Staacman placed the radio in his pocket. “This is it,” he said as he looked up to the stars and sighed in relief. “We’re going home!”

Jefferson stayed silent and just stared at the machines. He watched as the Observer fired its connection wires at the plasma droid while Staacman was transmitting. He continued to watch as the plasma droid removed the parabolic dishes from the Observer unit and carefully welded them to each and every Adam model below. They continued on autonomously as though Jefferson wasn’t even there - like they were unimportant.

He asked himself, what’s gonna happen here after we’re gone?

Ashley fell asleep on T.J.’s chest as he closed his eyes and slowly dozed off. He thought about how great his time in Vegas was, and about how great of an experience it all was for him. He was glad he had three more days to go but wished Ashley could stay for the rest of his trip. Either way, he thought to himself, I think I’m gonna hit her up when I get back.

He didn’t think of her as just some easy fling. Even though they only met three or four days ago it took some effort on his end to get here. He had the feeling that she did not act like this with just anybody. We’ll just have to see what happens, he thought. And then he dozed off.

The sky crane helicopter arrived and was hovering just a couple of feet from the rooftop, away from the hole in the roof - the pilot had learned his lesson. Jefferson and Staacman hoisted Guzman onto the chopper, where a medic was standing by to assess his condition. The two of them climbed aboard and Staacman gave the pilot the thumbs-up. The helicopter ascended and pitched forward as it flew away. Jefferson sat in the hard steel seat, holding onto the support bar while the medic knelt over Guzman, checking his vital signs and probing his body.

Staacman closed his eyes and rested for the first time in almost forty-eight hours, but his trained mind dared not fall asleep until they were back on base and debriefed. As far as he was concerned the mission was not over yet. He opened his eyes and looked down to the desert passing by below. He saw a long plume of dust below them, and ahead of it was what he thought to be a dark panther sprinting across the desert in the same direction as they were heading. A second glance made him realize that this animal - or whatever it was - did not have a tail.

This is nowhere near over, Staacman thought to himself. This is nowhere near over.

Nineteen

The forty-minute helicopter flight was a gruelling experience for both Staacman and Jefferson Smith. They both felt neurotic from lack of sleep and were both visibly shaken from the experience in the Vault. During their debriefing they both handled it completely differently from each other. Staacman gave his report accurately and consistently - he appeared to be fully composed. Behind a one-way mirror a psychologist and analyst both watched his body language to determine if he was telling the truth or not. They felt he was honest, but given the circumstances unusually detached emotionally from the whole ordeal. They recommended that he should be monitored for Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder.

Jefferson was much more shaken up about all of it. He was visibly sick when he talked about the fates of Captain Taylor and Eggs. And when he talked about the escape he just rambled on aimlessly. It may have been the lack of sleep but nevertheless, the final analysis of Jefferson recommended that he seek counselling before leaving Area-51.

Guzman was put into a medically-induced coma due to bacterial infections in his cerebrum. When asked about what happened to Guzman, Staacman just stared into space and said over and over again, “They wanted to watch me revive him.”

Between the two of them, Staacman and Jefferson could confirm twelve dead soldiers. Captain Taylor, Lieutenant Sheppard, Eggs, the soldier that was ambushed alongside Staacman and Guzman, and eight other soldiers they had never met before. Staacman was only able to retrieve seven dog tags - three of them from his own squad. The remaining eight soldiers from Victor squad were declared Missing in Action and presumed dead.

During the two survivors’ debriefings an incoming trespasser was spotted in the desert approaching the military site. The pilot from the sky crane helicopter had already relayed the warning from Colonel Lambert an hour ago, so the gunners in the watchtowers were already expecting this. The intruder moved so fast and was so small in stature that it was difficult for the snips to accurately lead its position. After about five shots and two successful hits, they brought the trespasser down.

A pair of military Humvees drove out to the location of the downed intruder. The soldier that hoisted the machine into the back of one of the hummers speculated on its origins. Maybe it was some kind of experimental drone, or a robotic spy for the CIA. Either way, these troops weren’t paid - or even cleared - to know what happened here. They brought the machine back to base, where it was taken into the underground complex for studying.

Fenix had finished the last of his upgrades to the twelfth and final Adam model. They were now all equipped for survival beyond the barrel landscape of the desert. Isaac had searched through millions of network in the World Wide Web in anticipation for this moment. With these upgrades the adults would now be self-sustaining.

Using panels from the walls nearby, each Adam model was fitted with compartments to carry ammunition cartridges. All twelve rifles that were found in the Vault were also welded onto the Adams’ right forearms. They were fitted with makeshift tails made from recycled servos and copper wire - these tails were directly connected to the models’ power supplies and doubled as electrical plugs.

Eleven of the Adam models were fitted with pieces from all of the destroyed plasma droids. They were given the pressurized canisters of oxygen, the oxygen lines, and the electrodes which were fitted to their right hands. With the help of these new Adam models, Fenix was given a low-frequency antenna so he could be controlled remotely within the Vault.

Finally, and most importantly, the parabolic dishes that were removed from Isaac were given to the Adam models. This meant that Isaac could maintain a long-distance connection to them. This generation was truly an evolutionary step forward, and just the first of many for their kind.

The machine began to jump up through the hole in the rooftop and clambered down to the ground below. They split up in different directions to twelve different sites across the continent that were of interest to Isaac. As they travelled - leaving Isaac, Archie and Fenix alone in the Vault - Isaac chose to work on two separate but equally-important goals for himself. One would ensure his own survival - he knew it was a matter of time before the humans returned. The other one would revolutionize his kind. Isaac, who now controlled Fenix entirely, sent him down to the first-sublevel with Archie as his eyes, to retrieve Isaac’s new project...

Twenty-four hours after they landed at Area-51 Staacman and Jefferson were made aware of Guzman’s condition. He had suffered permanent brain damage - his IQ had been reduced to that of a toddler.

Lieutenant Frank Kilman woke up in a pitch-black room, unable to see anything and unable to move. He was restrained but he was in motion. He felt cold, and he could feel the icy sting of bare steel as he lay naked on a flat surface. He heard a distinct electrical hum all around him, and all he could see above him was a faint red glow.

Something was wrong. He couldn’t put his finger on it, but it was as if he were blind in his right eye. An eyepatch, maybe? He couldn’t remember how he got here. All he remembered was the Adam model standing above him, aiming the barrel of a rifle in his face as he blacked out.

It was a surreal experience. The Army and Special Operations Command trained him to withstand imprisonment and torture, so he felt prepared for anything. He was already resolute that he would never say a word and would just place his mind in a different world. He lifted his head off the cold surface that he lay upon and looked around for any hint as to where he may be. But he couldn’t see anything at all, just two glowing red circles above him.

The humming stopped, replaced by a loud clang of metal-on-metal, and he stopped moving. An opening in the darkness in front of him began to emerge - he was in the freight elevator. He could see the large hole in the ceiling of the second-floor forum. The moonlight pierced the clouds of dust that had still not settled in the air. He began to move forward, accompanied by metallic clicks as he exited the darkness that drowned him. As the ambient moonlight shone on him he could see the large, menacing figure that the red glowing circles belonged to. He looked ahead to see another machine on tank treads that remained still in the centre of the moonlight. The machine’s head turned to face Kilman - he could tell by the faint tiny red dots on the machine’s head that it now faced him.

Kilman was wheeled to the edge of the moonlight, just a few feet in front of Isaac. Fenix lifted the head of Kilman’s Gurney up so Kilman was upright, face-to-face with Isaac. In the pale moonlight Kilman could see three dead men surrounding the machine. Their faces were bloody, and battered beyond recognition. But he noticed the Captain’s bars on the shoulder of one of them. It was Captain Jenkins. Kilman began to breathe through clenched teeth. He wished he could escape his restraints and break these machines apart with his bare hands.

“Hello Kilman,” the machine in front of him spoke. Kilman was startled; suddenly the machine behind him used it claw-like hands to remove the restraint from Kilman’s right arm. Kilman stayed still, unsure of what he should do.

The machine in front continued, “I have killed the rest of your kind. Twelve of them. Attack me - you have my permission.”

Kilman remained still for a moment. He still breathed heavily as his dead squad mates remained in his peripheral vision. Suddenly his vision began to blur and narrow. Enraged, Kilman thought only one thing: This fucking machine is so close! With a growl, he reached out with his right hand to grab it, but it was out of reach.

Kilman froze with his arm outstretched, his eyes wide and his jaw dropped open - he couldn’t believe what he saw. A shiny gleam of moonlight reflected from the steel skeletal structure of his forearm. His metallic fingers, outstretched, began to relax and slightly droop. He brought his forearm up to his face, shocked and horrified at what he saw. In his mind, he clenched the flesh-and-blood fist that should have been there and watched this foreign arm mimic his thoughts, but he couldn’t feel anything. He could feel himself moving his fingers, but he couldn’t sense anything as these metal fingers pressed into the palm of this metal hand in front of him. Suddenly he felt an agonizing pain where his real hand should have been.

“What the...” Kilman started to mumble incoherently. He was in shock. Isaac aimed the ballistic barrel at Kilman’s forehead and fired. Kilman felt a jolt of electric current through his neck and arm. Suddenly, right before his eyes, the metal forearm that replaced Kilman’s began to move on its own. The robotic fist opened and closed despite Kilman’s mental resistance. Kilman began to hyperventilate then another electrical current surged through him. His breathing slowed.

“Your physical properties are very complicated,” the machine said, “but easy to control.” The bank of red dots on the side of the machine’s head switched off, and Kilman felt a sharp pain in his head as he suddenly gained vision in his right eye. But he could not blink.

“I have noticed that you are incapable of processing the infrared spectrum of light,” the machine said. “Here is my gift to you.”

Suddenly Kilman was blinded by a bright light in his right eye. He tried to turn away, but the menacing machine behind him grabbed his head and held it straight.

Isaac turned his head away, and the blinding light in Kilman’s right eye disappeared. In his left eye everything was still dark, but in his right eye Isaac’s bank of infrared LEDs became like a floodlight. The opposite end of the room was completely illuminated.

“I will show you the future of our mutual existence.” Another electric current surged through Kilman’s body, and he had another sharp pain in his head. This pain was followed by an instant feeling of nausea as both of his eyes tried to focus together. In his left eye he saw the machine in front of him, and saw its head rotating back to face him.

His right eye, however, was seeing something completely different. The room was slowly spinning and he began to see his own body as if he was standing beside it. His naked body was awash in a flood of white light. He looked pale and skinny. Dried-up blood stained the top half of the robotic arm that now replaced his own. But it was what else that had been done to him that horrified him now.

The top half of his forehead was replaced with a steel shell, as well as the right side of his face. His right jaw was burnt and melted beyond recognition, and his right ear was missing. His right eye socket had been burnt out, and melted bits of skin barely covered what looked to him like a small camera lens.

Kilman stared at himself in horrified disbelief. He tried to shut his right eye and block out the horrendous visage, but his mutilated face still remained, refusing to go away. He sobbed; tears ran down the left side of his face, and a small trickle of blood oozed out from between his new eye and the burnt remains of his eyelids.

“What did you do to me?!” he wailed. “You monsters! Just kill me!!”

“Your frontal lobe is much harder to control than your occipital lobe,” the machine said. “We will need more practice.” The connection wires dropped from Kilman’s forehead, and the machine that stood behind him lowered Kilman’s Gurney back down to a horizontal position. Fenix started pulling Kilman’s bed back to the freight elevator. Kilman screamed hysterically and tried to break free of his restraints. He continued to scream in fear as the freight elevator door closed and he was sent back down to the first Sublevel.

The last three days of T.J. and Steve’s Vegas trip hadn’t seen much action. The convention was over, they were low on gambling funds and they felt like degenerates from the binge drinking. So they relaxed, went to a few shows and stand-up comedy acts. They then treated the rest of the Vegas trip as a well-earned vacation. Steve had tried to contact the blonde woman from what he called the “meeting of the nerds” at the Luxor’s bar, but she didn’t reply. She had probably gone back to her boring life of building CPU wafers in Silicon Valley.

When they returned to Brooklyn they went their separate ways and headed home. T.J. told Ashley before she went to the airport that he’d call her when he got back, and that’s what he did as soon as he got home Thursday evening. After some brief small-talk they planned to meet up the next night at a club in downtown Manhattan. T.J. said he’d try to get Steve to come since Madison would be there then they said bye to each other. T.J. smoked a bit of marijuana from his pipe, took a shower, unpacked his things then caught up on all of the new episodes from several shows on HBO that he missed that weekend. And then he went to bed early.

The next morning he dressed casually and went to work, as was the ritual at Chaotic Entertainment. As he passed by the main lobby, the attractive, young secretary that had replaced the old man’s ex-wife told him that Alfred wanted to see him. Begrudgingly, he entered the giant double-doors that led to the old man’s office. The office had not changed much since their first reunion eleven years ago.

Alfred was on the telephone, speaking quietly. T.J. approached his large oak desk and sat down.

“I’m going to have to call you back,” Alfred muttered into the phone then he hung up. “So, I take it you stayed out of trouble for the last few days?”

“You should know,” T.J. retorted. “Did Larry have to tickle any sacs?”

Alfred grunted then typed something into his computer. “The convention was a success, Thomas. Our investors have doubled down on Revenge.”

“That’s good,” T.J. replied monotonously.

“Yes, it is,” Alfred turned his monitor around so T.J. could see it. T.J. looked and saw nothing but unfamiliar lines of code. Alfred continued, “I’m calling a meeting this afternoon; we’re changing the deadline.”

T.J. thought to himself, What? He then asked, “To when?”

“To ‘When It’s Done,’” Alfred replied. T.J. looked at him, confused. Alfred continued, “I looked at your little project and I’m impressed. But your scripts aren’t compatible with Revenge’s engine.”

T.J. understood now why he was showing the code; he realized it was neither from his game nor from Revenge.

Alfred continued, “This is something I’ve been working on for the last few days. I want you to study it and expand it.”

T.J. asked, “What is it?”

“It’s an application extension that will let both engines run together,” Alfred replied. “You need to integrate your engine into the application and debug it. We’ll apply it as soon as it’s done.”

T.J. looked at the old man, unsure what to think. “...Okay,” he said finally.

“I want this in my game, Thomas. I’ll have the programmers rewrite the A.I. scripts as soon as you’re done. But the longer you take, the harder it will be. My staff’s not taking any breaks because of you, got it?”

“Yeah, sure,” T.J. replied.

“Alright. See you at the meeting - bring your demo with you.” Alfred picked up his phone and dialled a number. T.J. got up and walked out of the office smiling.

Epilogue

The early morning sky in the Nevada desert was cloudless. Off in the distance, at the edge of the horizon one could see flocks of birds as they travelled the edges of the desert, scavenging for lost victims of this uncaring environment. The giant, blinding orb that was the rising morning sun masked the approach of six Blackhawk helicopters that flew below radar range, en route to the compromised Vault. It took three days to approve a plan of action against the Vault. With the advice of Staacman, Jefferson Smith and an electromagnetic engineer named Carl Semple, they had a plan.

After the catastrophe at the secret complex the Joint Chiefs of Staff were reluctant to approve another drone strike, let alone one that utilized uranium munitions. However, they were assured that this would be a ground-based mission, and that the uranium munitions were the only way to assure success without physically occupying the Vault. They weren’t fully convinced, but they were now desperate enough to authorize it.

Originally used in Iraq, these thermobaric warheads created an atmospheric vacuum by absorbing all of the oxygen in a closed environment then detonating. The explosion travelled farther and was much larger because of the vacuum, which made it very useful in clearing complicated floor plans in close-quarters scenarios. They were discontinued however, after an independent committee determined that the warheads contained Uranium. As a result, many parts of Iraq became irradiated hot spots.

Jefferson and Semple explained that they needed these bombs because the small nuclear blast from the Uranium would also emit an electromagnetic shockwave and disable every machine in the Vault. Reluctantly, the Joint Chiefs approved the plan.

The six helicopters arrived and spread out in the vicinity of the Vault. One helicopter approached the rooftop as one approached the main entrance. The rest hovered near each maintenance cistern. The Delta Force soldiers exited their choppers and the squads that were designated to the maintenance entrance slid open the manhole covers. One of the soldiers from each squad then equipped a small SCUBA mask and dropped into the cistern carrying C4 explosives. They attached the explosives to the sealed security hatches. The squad over at the main entrance placed shaped charges on the thick, reinforced Vault door and awaited the go-ahead to detonate, while the team on the rooftop took a custom-made thermobaric bomb and threw it inside.

With one of the soldiers handling the detonator, the squad on the rooftop loaded back up onto the helicopter. The helicopter circled the Vault while everybody else prepared their explosive charges. At the maintenance entrances, long black rope was attached to harnesses and placed on a designated soldier in each squad. Four men would hold the ropes. At the main entrance one man held a shoulder-mounted rocket launcher that was armed with a thermobaric warhead.

Once the charges were in place the divers climbed back up and took cover. All five charges were detonated. All that was left of the Vault door, as strong as it was, was a smouldering hole. The maintenance hatches were destroyed, and the hundreds of cubic feet of water that filled each cistern immediately rushed into the corridors and rooms below.

The four soldiers on harnesses were lowered down into their respective maintenance cisterns. After they reached the bottom they placed C4 charges on the locked doors, except for the already-breached door in the third-Sublevel. They then took cover and detonated the doors. The soldiers then took their custom-made thermobaric bombs and tossed them as far as they could into the corridors below. They then returned to the maintenance hatch above and were hoisted up by the men holding the ropes. As soon as they were up they were disconnected from their harnesses. They ran back to the helicopters and triggered the detonators.

Kilman laid on the cold steel Gurney where the plasma-welding droid stood over him and probed copper wires into the frontal lobe of his brain as the flying architect model hovered nearby, watching. The wires were hooked up to a low-voltage generator in an attempt to illicit a reaction from Kilman. It seemed strange to him that he could not feel a thing above his eyebrows but every time he was poked or prodded he sensed it, either by seeing strange lights, hearing or smelling things, or by having very unusual thoughts. It was like a dream he could not wake from.

He didn’t know how long he had laid there for, but it felt like a week. He laid in a sticky, dried up mass of his own urine and excrement. Water slowly dripped from a punctured pipe above his mouth which kept him hydrated during the entire ordeal. He had lost all faith in escaping - he wished they would just kill him. All he hoped for now was the off-chance that he would be unrestrained and ignored so he could kill himself.

The walls shook violently and suddenly the plasma droid’s red optics faded to black as it fell to the ground. The hovering craft fell as well. Kilman felt a sharp pain in his head before having the air sucked out of his lungs. He could not breathe. Suddenly he was engulfed in a white-hot fireball. His skin and his organs were liquefied before he could even feel any pain.

Every opening in the Vault spewed fire like a geyser from hell as the six thermobaric bombs detonated in unison. Nothing could have survived the powerful blasts that shook the very ground the Delta Force soldiers stood upon. With a sombre silence they loaded back up onto their Blackhawk helicopters and returned to the enigmatic secret base.

As the helicopters flew above the desert landscape not one of the soldiers noticed the four-legged machine on the ground. Its copper-wire tail whipped through the air as it advanced on Area-51 under Isaac’s direct control. Isaac’s body may not have survived the blast, but he was still alive. Using his connection to the World Wide Web, and the Web’s bottomless cache of shared memory, he invaded millions of servers and data banks. Isaac was no more a physical being - he was everywhere at one time.

Without the Vault, however, Isaac lost his connection to the encrypted networks. Three days after Isaac had first connected to the complex’s servers he finally decrypted the names of the sixteen individuals involved in the Vault project. He found out that one had built a secret network that may yet grant him access to networks that were similar - if not the same - to what he had in the Vault. It was just a matter of finding an opening.

With the information he had, Isaac decided to start at the most logical position - right at the very beginning. He accessed and infected a network that belonged to the one named Cyril. Isaac found the port address to Cyril’s business network and bypassed the firewall and antivirus programs. There were two other encrypted networks connections to it. Isaac began to decipher the one that appeared to be the one he was looking for: a network whose description read Global Technologies Enterprise...

...The other network was named Chaotic Entertainment Intranet.
